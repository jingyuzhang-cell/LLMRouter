We evaluate collaborative scheduling on cross-document verification and report generation over public regulatory announcements. The main experiment executes a fixed DAG over four such announcements, with four extraction, two intermediate, one generation, and one deterministic formatting node; successors consume actual parent outputs. The pool combines Zhipu GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek deepseek-flash. Across six normal and controlled-anomaly scenarios (transient, structural, formatting, and latency faults), each strategy executes 32 times (192 DAG executions, 300-second deadline); executions are paired by scenario and replicate, not independent events. A separate batch tests 120, 150, and 250-second deadlines (35/36 recorded).

We compare Static (fixed allocation), Simple-Dynamic (uniform retry), R-only (predefined recovery including structure repair), pi-only (model switching when resources permit), Fixed-Composite (fixed repair and switching rules), and Proposed-Dynamic (anomaly-category action selection under the remaining budget). As R-only already repairs structure, this is a strategy-level comparison, not an orthogonal ablation of structure, recovery, and allocation. All strategies share initial states and budgets; differences reflect actions actually taken. An 89-request single-model batch examines automated planning and controlled evidence substitution; neither general structural advantage nor heterogeneous allocation is thereby validated; other strategies exclude substitution by action-space definition. Multi-objective search is assessed by replaying 15 recorded cooperative configurations (200 seeds, shared initial points, eight record queries) on logged measurements, not new executions.

Quality is scored against a frozen 23-slot fact contract (TP, TP/23); evidence analyses separate fact values, citations, and semantic support; machine references never count as human gold. We report calls, tokens, and estimated fees (uncertified settlements stay UNKNOWN); full-execution and recovery costs are separated; latency uses recorded wall clock, never summed service times as makespan; non-domination is compared only within task and caliber. The primary analysis compares Proposed-Dynamic with Simple-Dynamic on 16 paired TP differences in S3/S4 (one-sided exact sign-flip on signed ranks, alpha=0.05); zero differences stay in win/tie/loss counts but not in flips, leaving 15 effective pairs (8 for auxiliary Fixed-Composite). Failures, timeouts, and unscored outputs follow pre-specified rules without post-hoc removal. Replay uses hypervolume gap, budget-hypervolume AUC, and frontier recall with 10,000 paired permutations and Holm correction; this analysis is an archived recomputation, not a registered confirmation.
